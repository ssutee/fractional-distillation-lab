How does crude oil become petrol, jet fuel and roads?

Crude oil comes out of the ground as a thick, black mix of many different molecules. A refinery sorts them in a tall, hot tower. The trick: each kind of molecule turns into a gas and back into a liquid at its own temperature. This sorting method is called fractional distillation.

What is a hydrocarbon?

Almost everything in crude oil is a hydrocarbon: a molecule built from only two kinds of atoms, carbon and hydrogen. Build one below and see how its size decides where it ends up in the tower.

Carbon (C)

A carbon atom always makes 4 bonds, as if it had 4 hands. Carbon atoms hold hands with each other to build a chain.

Hydrogen (H)

A hydrogen atom makes just 1 bond, as if it had 1 hand. Hydrogen atoms fill every free hand on the carbon chain.

Tap a dot to build that molecule. Every extra carbon atom raises the boiling point.

Why do long chains boil at higher temperatures?

Molecules pull on each other a little, like weak magnets. Short chains touch their neighbours in only a few places, so a little heat is enough to pull them apart into a gas.

Long chains are like cooked spaghetti. They tangle and touch their neighbours all along their length, so it takes much more heat to pull them apart. That is why every extra carbon atom raises the boiling point.

Not every hydrocarbon is a straight chain

Crude oil also has hydrocarbons with branches and rings. They follow the same rule: bigger molecules boil at higher temperatures. This lab uses straight chains, called alkanes, because they are the easiest to count. The drawings show only the carbon atoms.

The one rule to remember

Most molecules in crude oil are chains of carbon atoms with hydrogen atoms stuck on. The length of the chain decides where the molecule ends up.

  • Short chain
  • Low boiling point
  • Stays a gas longer
  • Comes out high up
  • Long chain
  • High boiling point
  • Turns liquid sooner
  • Comes out low down

You have seen this at home

When water boils in a pot, steam rises and touches the cooler lid. The steam turns back into water drops. That change from gas to liquid is called condensing. The oil tower works the same way. Its trays act like lids at different temperatures, so different molecules condense on each one.

      Words to know

      Crude oil
      A thick, black liquid found deep underground. It formed over millions of years from the remains of tiny sea creatures.
      Hydrocarbon
      A molecule made only of hydrogen and carbon atoms. Crude oil is mostly hydrocarbons.
      Boiling point
      The temperature at which a liquid boils and turns into a gas. Bigger molecules have higher boiling points.
      Evaporate
      To change from a liquid into a gas, for example when it is heated.
      Condense
      To change from a gas back into a liquid, usually by cooling down.
      Fraction
      A group of molecules with similar sizes and boiling points that comes out of the tower together.
